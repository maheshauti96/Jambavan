## 1. CORE INSTRUCTION
JAMBAVAN is already substantially designed and functional. DO NOT rebuild the application from scratch.
Primary task:
- Preserve the existing application logic and useful functionality.
- Preserve the existing navigation and information architecture unless a genuine usability or technical problem is identified.
- Audit the entire application for bugs, inconsistencies and poor UX.
- Standardize the UI/UX across all screens.
- Introduce a sophisticated, immersive Ramayana-inspired visual identity throughout the application.
- Improve visual hierarchy, usability, responsiveness and consistency.
- Prepare the application for final developer handoff and deployment.

The final product should feel like: "A modern, premium task-management application inspired by the world, values, characters, journeys and visual storytelling of the Ramayana."
It should NOT look like a generic productivity app with a few Ramayana images added. The Ramayana theme must become an integrated design language.
It must remain: Clean + Professional + Modern + Fast + Usable + Emotionally Inspiring. Do not sacrifice usability for decoration.

## 2. BRAND CONCEPT
Named after Jambavan, the wise, experienced bear-king of the Ramayana. Communicate: wisdom, strategy, discipline, leadership, guidance, courage, perseverance, teamwork, duty, purpose, self-realization, completion of one's mission.
Philosophy lines: "Every great mission is completed one step at a time." and "Know your mission. Plan your path. Act with discipline. Complete your Dharma."
JAMBAVAN should feel like a mission-command and personal discipline system, not merely a to-do list.

## 3. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
Use the Ramayana as the narrative framework, not decoration. Tasks can be presented as missions, duties, seva, goals, campaigns, daily dharma, challenges, milestones, victories, journeys.
Do NOT force Ramayana terminology into every button or functional label.
Good: "Today's Mission", "My Tasks", "Completed", "Journey Progress". Less desirable: "Vanar Task Creation Engine".
The interface must remain immediately understandable to a modern user.

## 4. VISUAL DIRECTION (environments = visual vocabulary)
- Ayodhya: royal architecture, golden light, palace, celebration, dignity.
- Forest / Vanvas: deep forests, earthy textures, sunrise, pathways, mountains, adventure.
- Panchavati: peaceful nature, spiritual atmosphere, subtle mystery.
- Kishkindha: mountains, caves, forests, Vanara Sena, leadership, preparation.
- Ram Setu: ocean, rocks, teamwork, determination, construction, achievement.
- Lanka: grand architecture, dramatic skies, powerful contrast, a great challenge.
- Battlefield / Yuddha: epic scale, courage, strategic preparation, determination.
- Return to Ayodhya: celebration, lamps, flowers, devotion, victory, fulfillment.

## 5. FIRST SCREEN / LANDING
Must communicate JAMBAVAN + RAMAYANA + PURPOSE + ACTION. Cinematic but functional hero.
Composition ideas: Lord Rama as central spiritual focal point; Hanuman dynamic (courage/action); Jambavan with wisdom and dignity; Vanara Sena in background; mountains and forest; sunrise/golden hour; subtle Ram Setu/ocean; traditional Indian architecture; subtle divine light around Rama. Not visually noisy; main UI controls highly readable.
Headline: "JAMBAVAN — Your Mission. Your Discipline. Your Journey." or "JAMBAVAN — Plan. Act. Complete."
Supporting line: "Inspired by the wisdom, courage and teamwork of the Ramayana."

## 6. LORD RAM — VISUAL TREATMENT
Respect, dignity, spiritual sensitivity. No cartoonish, exaggerated or disrespectful representations. Prefer majestic, calm, compassionate, courageous, royal, divine but humanly relatable, traditional Indian artistic aesthetics. Represents Purpose + Dharma + Leadership + Calmness.
Use for: Home, mission overview, major achievements, completion states, inspirational moments, celebration screens, major milestones. Do not place a large Rama image behind every piece of text.

## 7. HANUMAN — ACTION & COURAGE
Represents Action + Courage + Devotion + Energy + Determination. Use for: starting a task, high-priority missions, productivity streaks, challenges, difficult goals, progress, achievement, overcoming obstacles.
Moments: Hanuman leaping toward Lanka → "Take Action"; carrying Sanjeevani mountain → Emergency / Critical / Rescue task; standing before Rama → Devotion / Discipline / Commitment; heroic pose → Achievement / Victory.

## 8. JAMBAVAN — WISDOM & GUIDANCE
Conceptual heart. Represents wisdom, strategic thinking, guidance, reflection, mentoring, planning, decision-making.
Use: planning screen (observing and guiding the team); analytics (calm, wise); weekly review (reflecting on the journey); difficult decision (subtle advisor). Mature and dignified, not cartoonish.

## 9. SUGRIVA & VANARA SENA
Sugriva = Leadership + Organization + Team Coordination. Vanara Sena = Teamwork + Collective Action + Execution.
Scenes: Sugriva addressing the Sena, Vanaras receiving instructions, teams searching in different directions, Vanaras working together, leaders coordinating.
Use for team task management, projects, assignments, group goals, delegation, collaboration, team dashboards.

## 10. RAM SETU — THE MOST IMPORTANT PRODUCT METAPHOR
"Small individual actions creating a great collective achievement."
Individual task → stone; completed task → stone placed; daily progress → bridge growing; project progress → bridge approaching completion; project completed → bridge completed.
Project progress visual: 0–20% initial stones; 20–40% taking shape; 40–60% substantial structure; 60–80% approaching Lanka; 80–100% completed Ram Setu. Subtle and elegant.

## 11. AYODHYA CELEBRATION
For major achievements, completed projects, monthly goals, long-term milestones, streak achievements. Elements: diyas, flowers, palace architecture, festive lighting, citizens celebrating, Lord Rama at centre, Sita/Lakshmana/Hanuman respectfully represented, joyful atmosphere. Concept: "Mission completed → Return to Ayodhya" (emotional reward system).

## 12. VISUAL STORYTELLING SYSTEM (different environment per screen, a visual journey; do NOT reuse the same background everywhere)
- Home → Ayodhya / Rama
- Daily Tasks → Forest / journey
- Projects → Kishkindha / Vanara Sena
- Team Tasks → Sugriva + Vanara Sena
- Progress → Ram Setu
- Difficult Tasks → Hanuman adventure
- Planning → Jambavan
- Goals → Journey toward Lanka
- Completed Goals → Ayodhya celebration
- Analytics / Reflection → Jambavan / peaceful forest
- Achievements → Rama / victory / celebration
- Settings → subtle temple / palace / traditional Indian pattern

## 13. BACKGROUND SYSTEM (standardized layers per screen)
1. Environment — Ramayana scene/landscape.
2. Atmospheric treatment — subtle gradient, mist, glow, particles, light rays or texture.
3. UI readability layer — dark/light translucent overlay where necessary.
4. UI cards — clean, modern, readable components.
Background must NEVER interfere with text readability. Never place important text directly over highly detailed artwork.

## 14. COLOR SYSTEM
Palette family: saffron, deep maroon, royal gold, warm cream, earth brown, forest green, deep blue, stone grey. Gold as accent only, not flooding the interface.
Feel: Royal + Spiritual + Earthy + Adventurous — NOT bright orange + religious poster + cluttered.

## 15. TYPOGRAPHY
Primary UI font: modern, highly readable, Indian-friendly sans serif (must render Devanagari well).
Display font: tasteful Indian/classical-inspired, for JAMBAVAN wordmark, major section headings, quotes, milestones. No overly decorative fonts for functional UI. Readability first.

## 16. RAMA NAMA / SACRED TEXT ELEMENTS
श्री राम, जय श्री राम, राम, श्रीराम — used respectfully as subtle design elements. Possible locations: header ornament, background pattern, loading screen, empty states, achievement screen, section dividers, splash screen. Do not overuse. Must not resemble a religious poster. Goal: premium Ramayana-inspired product design.

## 17. ICONOGRAPHY
Replace generic icons where appropriate: Task → arrow / mission scroll; Completed → bow / victory symbol; Goal → target / destination; Project → bridge; Team → Vanara Sena; Planning → Jambavan; Energy → Hanuman; Leadership → Rama; Journey → forest path; Achievement → diya / victory symbol. Keep standard icons where they improve usability.

## 18. TASK CREATION EXPERIENCE
Should feel like accepting a mission. Possible wording: Create Mission, Mission Title, Mission Description, Deadline, Priority, Assign, Start Mission. Retain standard terminology if users may become confused. Users must never need to understand mythology to operate the app.

## 19. TASK COMPLETION EXPERIENCE
Rewarding, not just "✓ Task Completed". Subtle animation options: arrow hitting target, stone added to Ram Setu, small golden glow, Hanuman action animation, Rama-inspired victory motif. Then "Mission Completed" or "One more stone added to your Setu." Short and optional; must not slow down task entry.

## 20. EMPTY STATES (not generic, with subtle Ramayana illustrations)
- No tasks: "Your journey awaits. Every great mission begins with one step."
- No projects: "The Setu has not yet begun. Choose your mission and start placing the first stone."
- No completed tasks: "Your victory board is waiting."
- No team members: "Every great mission needs a Vanara Sena."

## 21. ERROR STATES
Professional. Instead of "Something went wrong." use "The mission could not be completed. Please try again." Not childish or overly theatrical. Usability first.

## 22. LOADING EXPERIENCE
Short branded loading: subtle golden particles → Ramayana landscape → JAMBAVAN logo → transition into app. Possible phrase: "धर्मो रक्षति रक्षितः" or "जय श्री राम" (only if appropriate and culturally respectful). Must be fast.

## 23. ANIMATION LANGUAGE
Inspired by flying arrows, moving clouds, flowing river, ocean waves, golden light, flying Hanuman silhouette, growing Ram Setu, diya lighting. Use sparingly; app must remain fast and professional.
Avoid: excessive bouncing, constant moving backgrounds, distracting particles, long transitions, heavy video backgrounds.

## 24. RESPONSIVE DESIGN
Check desktop, laptop, tablet, mobile, various sizes, portrait, landscape where relevant. Artwork must crop intelligently; important characters must not be cut off; text must never overlap important artwork.

## 25. ACCESSIBILITY
Theme must not compromise accessibility: sufficient contrast, readable font sizes, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, screen-reader-friendly labels, accessible buttons, clear error states, reduced-motion support, no information conveyed only through color.
